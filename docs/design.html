Design guidelines

How Dashi looks, moves, and speaks

One page for everything people see: the name and the mark, the colors and what each one means, type, spacing, windows, motion, icons, the components apps are built from, the patterns they follow, the voice, and Kombu. Values are read from the code, and each section names its file. Change one, and change it here in the same commit.

How to use this page

  • Rules first. Each section ends in numbered rules (CO-2, PA-1). Cite them in reviews and commits. The explanation above them is short on purpose.
  • Tags tell you how true a rule is today. A rule with no tag holds in the code. The others are written down so they stop drifting.
  • The code wins a disagreement until someone fixes one side. If the page and the code differ, what ships is the code: fix the page, or fix both together.
  • Where the tagged rules came from: a review of every first-party app in November 2026 (the "roasts"). A tag names the roast and the place in the code; the reports are in docs/roast/, and their proposals in proposals.html.

Vertical space is sacred.

One chrome row per window. No second bars, no two-line titles. This is the owner's rule and it outranks every other on the page: when a design needs more room for chrome, the design changes, not the rule. The details →

Principles

Calm by default

Nothing moves, flashes, or asks for attention unless the person needs it now. Quiet surfaces, one accent, gentle motion.

Content first, chrome recedes

One slim row of chrome, glass and hairlines instead of heavy frames. Toolbars merge into windows; the menu bar and Dock let the wallpaper through.

Familiar, then delightful

Start from what people know from macOS and Windows, so nothing has to be learned. Then add the touches that make it Dashi's: the Shapes, the ingredients, Kombu.

One color, one job

Kombu means "yours, selected"; ume means "close or destroy"; broth means "set aside or careful"; shiso means "an agent is acting". Never reuse a color for a second meaning.

Everything from the same tokens

Shell, apps, the login screen, and GTK apps all draw from one palette and one set of measures. Rust draws chrome from the same values the shell sends; no surface invents its own.

Motion explains

A window flies to the Dock tile it went to; a Desktop slides the way it lies. Motion shows where things went, quickly, and stops entirely with Reduce Motion.

Nothing is lost by accident

What can't be undone asks first, and what can be undone says how. Closing, quitting, and moving things never loses work silently.

Vertical space

A laptop screen is wide and short. Every row of chrome is a row of someone's work, so a window gets exactly one: the 28 px title bar, or the app's 38 px unified toolbar. Everything that wants to live in a second row finds a home in that one, in the sidebar, or in the content.

  1. VS-1
    One chrome row per window: the title bar or the unified toolbar, with nothing stacked under it.Not yet in codeTerminal roast · its tab bar is a second 28 px row (apps/terminal/tab-bar.tsx)
  2. VS-2
    Titles are one line. A count or a scope that belongs to the title follows it on the same line, muted, after an em dash ("All Notes — 3 notes", AppWindow's detail); anything longer goes in the sidebar or the content. AppWindow takes no subtitle, and a toolbar control never wraps to a second line.
  3. VS-3
    No footer bar. Something that is happening (progress, an error with its action) is a notice that floats over the foot of the content (AppWindow's notice, @dashi/ui's Notice), takes no room from it, and leaves when it's done or dismissed. Name and mark

Dashi is the stock Japanese cooking starts from: kombu and bonito simmered into something you barely notice and everything depends on. The OS aims for the same: a calm foundation that makes everything on it taste better.

The name

Say it

DAH-shee

出汁 · Japanese soup stock

Write it

Dashi: one word, capital D. In system UI and docs it is just "Dashi", never "the Dashi".

Not DASHI, DashiOS, Dashi OS, or dashi (except in code, package, and command names: dashi-comp, ID=dashi). dashiOS is only the repository's name.

Inspired, not imitated

Dashi takes what people already love about macOS and Windows and makes it its own. The test for every borrowed idea: would someone who knows both feel at home, and would nobody mistake it for either?

FromWhat Dashi takesHow it becomes Dashi's

⚠︎

Never ship another company's assets. No Apple or Microsoft icons, glyphs (⌘, ⊞), fonts (SF, Segoe), wallpapers, sounds, palettes, or product names in the UI. Reinterpret the idea, then draw it fresh. A code comment that says "as on the Mac" is a cue to check that Dashi reinterpreted, not copied.

The mark

A bowl and three wisps of steam: dashi, freshly made. The app tile sets it in white on a kombu gradient; the glyph alone works wherever a single color is needed.

  • Tile: 44 × 44 on a 48 grid, corner radius 10.5, filled top to bottom from #5FAE95 to #2F6F5A, with a 1.5 white inner line at 18%: the same recipe as every Dashi app icon (Icons).
  • Glyph: drawn on a 16-unit grid: the bowl a half-ellipse 13 wide, three wisps 3 apart, 1.3 strokes with round caps. Near-white #F4F4F6 on the tile.
  • Clear space: an eighth of the tile on every side (the dashed line). Nothing else enters it.
  • Smallest size: 16 px for the tile, 12 px for the glyph. Below that the wisps merge.
  • Files: packages/shell/icons/setup.svg (tile), spin/dashi-logos/ (PNG and Plymouth frames).
Dashi

The lockup: the wordmark is Inter Bold at −0.02em tracking, its cap height about half the tile's. The gap between them is a quarter of the tile. Use the lockup where the name isn't already nearby; inside the OS, the mark alone is enough.

Color

Ingredients

Dashi's colors are named after what goes into the pot. Each has one job; select one to copy its hex.

Neutrals: ink #1D1D1F and paper #F5F5F7, swapped in Dark Mode. Success uses moss #4F9A3E, a green deliberately not the accent. New colors get an ingredient's name and one job, or they don't get in. The brand values above are for marks and fills; text in a job color uses its ink token (ume, broth, moss), a shade of the same ingredient that is AA in each mode (see Contrast below).

Tokens

Two sets, light and dark, that fill the same names (packages/dashi-ui/src/theme/palette.ts). Translucent values are glass: they assume a wallpaper or window behind. Select a swatch to copy its value.

Translucent swatches show a checkerboard through them.

Tints

Color that only tells things apart: Settings' sidebar tiles and Setup's headings, calendar colors, store categories, the letter that stands in for a missing icon, Activity Monitor's graph series. One list for all of them (packages/dashi-ui/src/theme/tints.ts), named for more of the kitchen, and none of them a job color, so a tile never says "selected", "destroy", "warning", "done" or "agent". Agents' own tiles are the one exception: they wear shiso because they are about agents. White glyphs and text read on every tint, at least 3.5:1, in both modes. Select one to copy it.

Calendars take them in this order, mizu first; a color an imported calendar brings stays. There is no red among them: red is for deleting.

Color rules

  1. CO-1
    One color, one job. Kombu: selected, on, the one primary action. Ume: close, destroy, error. Broth: warning, set aside. Moss: success. Shiso: agents. Nothing else gets a job color.
  2. CO-2
    Kombu never decorates: not for hover, not for icons at rest, not for a category or a whole group of controls. Sidebar glyphs are ink or muted; only the selected row is kombu.Not yet in codeFiles roast · SidebarItem's glyphs are muted, and so are Files' sidebar glyphs and the file chooser's file and folder glyphs (ink on a selected row); Open With rows (Preview, Files, the chooser) show each app's own icon and hover in a wash. Calculator's keys follow it: kombu only on = and the operator waiting for a number. Some links are still kombu text (the consent sheet's "Other permissions", the print sheet's "More…")
  3. CO-3
    Shiso appears only where an agent is acting or asking, and on what is about agents (Settings' Agents tile, Setup's Agents page). No category, tile or tint wears it otherwise. In the launcher: the Ask row's glyph and words (agentInk), the plan's scope chips, the field's spark on a plan, and a 6 px dot on each "Now" row's icon. A selected agents' row is still kombu: selecting is the person's (launcher/row-view.tsx, launcher/agents/plan-view.tsx).
  4. CO-4
    No palette copied from another platform. Decorative tints (Settings tiles, calendar colors, store categories, chart series) come from one Dashi-made list that leaves out the job colors: Tints.
  5. CO-5
    Tokens only. No hex in apps or the shell: grounds come from Surface, inks from Palette. A new need adds a token to palette.ts and to this page in the same commit. Code asks the palette whether it is dark; it never compares palette objects.Not yet in codeTerminal, Preview roasts · Terminal's palette is its own (apps/terminal/palette.ts), and its tab shades are hex (apps/terminal/view.tsx); Preview's canvas ground is hex (apps/preview/canvas.tsx); an identity check remains in Appearance's previews; Software's pill ink and Flathub's verified seal are tokens now (accentInk, verified) (settings-app/panes/appearance.tsx)
  6. CO-6
    Contrast: text is at least 4.5:1 against its ground, muted text and white-on-kombu included; control edges (buttons, fields, checkboxes, an off switch) are at least 3:1. Check both modes before merging; the tokens' ratios are in Contrast.Not yet in code@dashi/ui roast · the tokens and Button pass; text fields, checkboxes, and the off switch (dashi-ui/src/switch.tsx) have no 3:1 edge yet. The selection meets it (CO-9): onSelection ink on selectionWash is at least 11.2:1 light and 10.4:1 dark over the launcher's glass, whatever the wallpaper, and at least 11.8:1 light and 9.9:1 dark on every window ground; its bar is at least 3:1 against the ground beside the row (dashi-ui/test/selection-contrast.test.ts)
  7. CO-7
    Error and destructive text uses one token in both modes: ume (critical and batteryLow are the same value). A destructive button is umeFill under white text.
  8. CO-8
    Every window follows the shell's Dark Mode from its first frame. No app has its own theme switch. A recessed field is darker than its window in both modes.Not yet in code@dashi/ui, Calendar roasts · Components opens dark on a light desktop; surface.field is lighter than the window in Dark Mode (surface.ts:22)
  9. CO-9
    One selection look. A selected row in a list, a table or a sidebar, in the launcher or an app, sits on selectionWash with a 3 px selectionBar at its left edge (20 tall, or the row's height less 6 above and below), and every word and glyph on it is onSelection ink. No row is solid kombu under white: @dashi/ui's rowLook and SelectionBar draw it (dashi-ui/src/selection.tsx), and SidebarItem and Table use them, as do Notes' list, Files' sidebar, list, icons and path suggestions, Calendar's calendars, the file and app choosers, Force Quit, and Settings' pickers. An icon view washes the whole tile and draws no bar. Selected text in a field sits on the same kombu, washed deeper since it has no bar (textSelection: at least 1.3:1 from every ground a field sits on, in both modes, where a row's wash is 1.29:1 on a Dark card), under the field's own ink, while the field has the keyboard; a password field's dots are washed too (TextSelectionLook in dashi-ui/src/selection.tsx, around every surface's tree, gives each TextInput mirin's selectionColor; dashi-ui/test/selection-contrast.test.ts). The caret is a thin bar in the field's ink, drawn on the boundary between two characters: it takes no room, so no text moves when it or a selection does. A menu's highlighted item is not a selection: it follows the pointer and stays kombu under white (5.1:1).

Typography

Inter everywhere (INTERFACE_FAMILY, appearance/look.ts), with Noto Sans for scripts Inter lacks and Noto Sans Mono for code. GTK apps get "Inter 11". The compositor's window titles are SemiBold 13. Every specimen below is editable.

  • Weights: Regular for reading, SemiBold (600) for labels that head something, Bold (700) for titles and the menu bar's app name. No Light, except the boot splash's title.
  • Muted text is the ink at 65% in light mode and 60% in dark (textMuted), not a separate gray, so it works on any glass and stays AA on every window ground.
  1. TY-1
    The scale is 11 (details, captions, section headings), 12 (secondary text, sheet messages), 13 (body, labels, toolbar and window titles), 15 (an empty state's title), 17 (the launcher's field, the one size it adds), 20 (a page title), and 88 for the clock. No other sizes, no fractional ones. The launcher: its field 17, a row's name 13, what follows it 12, its kind and the section heads 11 (launcher.tsx, launcher/row-view.tsx). Text size (Settings › Appearance: Default, Large, Larger, Largest, 1, 1.15, 1.3 and 1.5 times) multiplies the launcher's sizes, each rounded to a whole pixel (launcher/measures.ts), and reaches GTK apps as GNOME's text scaling factor (text-size.ts).Not yet in code@dashi/ui, Settings roasts · the shell uses 20 sizes, including 11.5, 12.5 and 13.5; Text size reaches the launcher and GTK apps only; Dashi's own windows keep their sizes (launcher/measures.ts is its one reader)
  2. TY-2
    Three weights: 400, 600, 700.

Shadows

Two, from mirin Metal's presets: sm (0 1 3 0 and 0 1 2 −1, both 10%) for controls and cards, xl (0 20 25 −5 and 0 8 10 −6, both 10%) for sheets and menus. Windows cast their own, drawn by the compositor (below); a sheet attached to a window casts xl from there too.

Window depth

Every window has a 1 px hairline just inside its frame and a soft shadow, deeper on the one with the keyboard, so two windows of the same color separate on any wallpaper and in both modes. Appearance › Highlight the active window (off by default) puts a kombu ring around the focused window in place of its hairline. There is no dimming: the window you read from keeps its colors. The shell decides the look (packages/shell/src/appearance/window-style.ts) and the compositor draws it, with one shader for the shadow and the lines (crates/dashi-comp/src/render/depth/). Compare:

Full screen gets its own Desktop

As on a Mac, a window in full screen moves to a Desktop of its own, so a game or a video sits one swipe away from everything else (packages/shell/src/spaces/fullscreen.ts; Desktop & Dock › "Give full-screen apps their own desktop", settings.json spaces.fullscreenDesktop, on by default; turned off, full screen stays on the Desktop it is on).

Rules

  1. WI-1
    The square never hides the menu bar by surprise. Pressing it fills the usable area, menu bar and Dock still there; pressing it again, or on a tiled window, restores the window's own frame. ⌥ and the square enters full screen; double-clicking the title bar does what the square does.
  2. WI-2
    ⌃❖ and an arrow tiles to that half: ← left, → right, ↑ top, ↓ bottom. Fill (⌃❖↩), Center (⌃❖C), and Back to Previous Size (⌃❖R) keep their own keys, listed in the Window menu.
  3. WI-3
    The keys, drag-to-tile, and the Window menu offer the same tiles, and the menu shows each tile's key.
  4. WI-4
    Full screen is always there for the person who asks: ⌃❖F, the Window or View menu, and an app's own button or F11.
  5. WI-5
    A tiled or filled window keeps the frame it had before, and Back to Previous Size gives it back; the row is disabled when there is nothing to go back to. Moving or resizing the window by hand ends the tile.
  6. WI-6
    A full-screen window has a Desktop to itself, next to the one it came from, and goes back there when it leaves full screen. Entering and leaving are each one motion: the window changes size where it is and stays on screen while the Desktops slide under it (space-switch {carry}); no Desktop is ever shown without it, and no picture of it at the wrong size.
  7. WI-7
    Nothing but the app's own dialogs and sheets opens over a full-screen window; any other window opens on the Desktop it came from, and the screen goes there to show it.
  8. WI-8
    A window's controls never go missing. A unified window keeps them in full screen, and every picture of a window, small or moving, shows its controls or title bar shrunk with it.

Patterns

Confirm and undo

  1. PA-1
    Irreversible means confirm or undo, never neither. A confirmation names what will be lost ("Delete "Groceries"? You can't undo this."), and a row's destructive action confirms in place, as Network and Bluetooth do for Forget. Use @dashi/ui's ConfirmSheet with danger, or useConfirm(): confirm.ask({ title, message, confirmLabel, danger, onConfirm }) from the handler and confirm.sheet in the window's overlay.
  2. PA-2
    Moving, renaming, and trashing offer ❖Z, and a notice with Undo. Every app's Undo is @dashi/ui's stack: push an action with do, undo, and a name once it is done, and Edit's rows say "Undo Rename" and "Redo Rename" from that name (undoMenuItems; Text Editor's own history uses the same labels). Actions pushed together are one step, and the same setting changed again soon after joins its step.dashi-ui/src/undo.ts · Files (apps/files/undoable.ts: rename, move, Move to Trash, new folder, copies), Notes (a deleted note), Calendar (a new, moved, edited, or deleted event), Settings (every change to a setting is an entry of its change log, settings-app/changes/: Recently changed lists each with who made it, you or an agent by name, and Undo; ❖Z undoes the newest)
  3. PA-3
    A name collision always asks: Replace, Keep Both, or Skip.Not yet in codeFiles roast · copies silently become "name 2"
  4. PA-4
    Closing never loses work: a document with changes is saved, or kept for next time, or the close asks. A tab with a running program asks before it closes. However the window is closed (the close control, ❖W, ❖Q, the Dock's Quit, logging out), the app gets the close as a request: with nothing to lose it closes at once, else it holds the window and asks with one sheet. The question says which: ❖Q, Quit (in the app's menu or the Dock's), and logging out ask “Quit Terminal?”, the close control “Close this window?”. Logging out, restarting, and shutting down ask the windows one at a time, the next once the last has gone, so two apps’ questions are never up together; they wait for the answers, and a notice names the app that holds them in one line (“Waiting for “Text Editor”. It may be asking first.”; a longer name drops the second sentence, and three apps or two long names are counted), with Cancel and a way to go anyway. It never covers the app's question: an app's sheet hangs from its window's top, so the notice floats at the foot of the screen above the Dock, as a window's notice does (VS-3), and takes no row (one chrome row); only when the window sits so low that its sheet would reach the foot does it float under the menu bar instead.shell/apps/kit/close.ts (mirin's MIRIN_METAL_CLOSE=ask; the shell's {"type":"quit"} on the menus channel says a close is a quit) · Text Editor asks Save, Don't Save, or Cancel; Terminal names what still runs · shell/src/closing-apps.ts, shell/src/menus/holding-place.ts (the notice: a card at most 560 × 48, 28 of shadow room, its line at most 50 characters, HOLDING_LINE; a sheet reaches at most 300 below its window's top)

Voice and copy

Dashi speaks like a good host: calm, plain, and brief. It tells you what will happen, not how it works, and it never shouts.

Calm

Plain

Everyday words over jargon: "Window corners", not "Decoration radius". Name things for what people see.

Brief

One sentence where one will do. Details under a label are full sentences, with a period.

Kind

Never blame the person. Ask before anything that can't be undone, and say exactly what will be lost.

Kombu

A small sprite of kelp, the leaf that dashi is simmered from, with the logo's wisp of steam curling above it. Kombu is curious, patient, and a little shy: it shows up when the OS has a moment to be warm, then gets out of your way.

Made of

A kombu leaf, flat in the accent, with two dot eyes and the mark's single wisp of steam as a cowlick.

Personality

Quietly delighted. Patient with mistakes. Never smug, never loud, never in a hurry.

Job

Keeps people company where the OS is empty or new: first launch, empty states, the docs, release notes.

Faces

One body, six faces. Each has a job; don't invent new ones without adding them here.

Where Kombu appears

You're all setThe last page of Setup Assistant.
No notes yetAn empty state, in an app's own window.
Looking for updates…A wait long enough to need company.
  • Setup Assistant's welcome and its last page
  • Empty states in first-party apps (no notes, no files, no results)
  • Long, friendly waits (updates, installing)
  • The docs, release notes, the website, stickers
  • An error page that isn't urgent ("This page couldn't load")
  • System chrome: the menu bar, the Dock, window controls, menus
  • The login and lock screens: they belong to the person
  • Alerts about data loss, security, or consent (agents use shiso, never Kombu)
  • As an app icon or in place of the mark
  • Anywhere Reduce Motion is on, animated

Drawing Kombu

  • Smallest: 24 px tall. Below that the face is unreadable; use the mark instead. Below 32 px, keep to the happy face.
  • Clear space: a quarter of Kombu's width around it, and never cropped by an edge.
  • Ground: calm, light or dark. On a wallpaper, set Kombu on a glass card, not on the picture.
  • Dark Mode: the body lightens a step (#5FAE95) so it doesn't sink into dark glass. The drawing does this for you.
  • Body: one closed drop-shaped path on a 122 × 150 grid, one flat fill in the accent (#3E8A72). No gradient, highlight, outline, or shadow.
  • Face: #123A2F. Eyes are dots (or 3-weight arcs when closed or smiling), the mouth one round 3-weight stroke. Nothing else: no blush, no catchlights.
  • The wisp: the mark's wisp of steam, the body's top color, 3.2 strokes with round caps.
  • Motion: a slow idle bob (8 px over 3.2 s, ease-in-out) and a quick 120 ms cross-fade between faces. It never bounces, spins, or moves across the screen, and with Reduce Motion it holds still.
  • Source: kombuSprite() in docs/assets/docs.js; when Kombu lands in the OS, it moves to @dashi/ui and this page draws from there.
  • Use the faces as drawn
  • Keep it small next to text, larger on its own
  • Let it react to what the person did (cheer when setup finishes)
  • Recolor it, outline it, or give it props and outfits (seasonal art may, in the docs and website only)
  • Make it speak in the first person in system UI
  • Use it to soften bad news that needs the person's full attention

Wallpapers

Drawn from formulas, not photographs (packages/shell/wallpapers/generate.ts): 3840 × 2160 with a 2 × 2 Bayer dither, about 1.5 MB each. Dusk is the default and matches the compositor's built-in gradient, so a fresh session never flashes.
